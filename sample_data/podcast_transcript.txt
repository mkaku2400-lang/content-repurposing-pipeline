Small Batch - Episode 41: The Clipboard List
Host: Maya Ortiz. Guest: Daniel Reyes, co-founder of Kettle Lane Coffee.

[00:00] MAYA: Welcome back to Small Batch, the show about small businesses that grew without a marketing department. Today I'm with Daniel Reyes. He and his wife Ana run Kettle Lane Coffee, a roastery with four thousand subscribers and, as far as I can tell, no advertising at all. Daniel, is that right?

[00:24] DANIEL: That's right. We spent money on ads once. It went badly. We can get into that.

[01:45] MAYA: Let's start at the start. Where was Kettle Lane in 2019?

[01:52] DANIEL: In our garage. We had a five-pound roaster, and every Saturday we sold bags at the farmers market. A good Saturday was sixty bags. That was the whole business.

[05:10] MAYA: And then the market closed.

[05:14] DANIEL: March 2020, it shut with a week's notice. What we had was a clipboard. For about a year Ana had been asking people at the stall to write down their email if they wanted to know when a new roast came in. Most weeks nobody did anything with it. When the market closed there were two hundred and twelve addresses on that clipboard, and it was the only way we had to reach anybody.

[08:30] MAYA: So what did you send them?

[08:34] DANIEL: A plain email. No design, no logo. I wrote, here's the coffee we roasted this week, here's the farm it came from, and if you want a bag, reply to this email and we'll drop it at your door. Thirty-eight people ordered from that first one. So we did it again the next Sunday. We have sent one every Sunday since, and it is still plain text.

[10:02] MAYA: Still reply-to-order?

[10:05] DANIEL: No, that broke at around three hundred orders a week. Now there's a subscription, thirty-two dollars a month for two bags. But the email is the same idea. One coffee, one story.

[12:20] MAYA: Tell me about the ads.

[12:24] DANIEL: In 2021 everyone told us we had to be on Instagram. So we ran ads for three months. We spent eighteen hundred dollars and got eleven customers out of it, and most of them cancelled within two months. They'd signed up for a discount, they didn't know us. Meanwhile the email list was adding forty or fifty people a week from customers forwarding it to friends. So we stopped the ads and never went back.

[15:05] MAYA: You also don't discount. Why not?

[15:09] DANIEL: Because the people who come for a discount leave for a discount. What we do instead is first dibs. Subscribers get every new roast forty-eight hours before anyone else. Some of the small lots sell out inside that window, so being on the list is the only way to get them. It costs us nothing and it's worth more to people than ten percent off.

[18:40] MAYA: What about people cancelling? Every subscription business has that problem.

[18:45] DANIEL: Early on we were losing nine percent of subscribers a month. When we asked why, the most common answer was, I still have coffee left from last time. They didn't dislike us, they were just full. So we added a button, skip a month. One click, no questions, no are-you-sure page. Churn went from nine percent to four.

[21:15] MAYA: What's the biggest mistake you made?

[21:19] DANIEL: Buying the big roaster too early. In 2022 we took a forty thousand dollar loan for a machine sized for a business three times ours. The repayments nearly sank us that winter. We should have rented time on someone else's roaster for another year.

[24:30] MAYA: If someone listening has a small business and no audience, what do they do on Monday?

[24:36] DANIEL: Pick one channel and be boring about it for a year. Ours was a Sunday email. And reply to people yourself. I personally answered every email from our first thousand customers. That's where the subscription idea came from, and the skip button, and most of what works.

[26:50] MAYA: Daniel Reyes, Kettle Lane Coffee. Thank you for coming on.

[26:54] DANIEL: Thanks for having me.
